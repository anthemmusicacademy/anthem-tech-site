Business founder first development

Most software is built by people who have never run the business it serves. This was built by the person who does, for owner-operators and small teams that can't afford to slow down.

1Built by someone who runs the business

Every feature started as a real bottleneck hit at the front desk, in the classroom or on the books. Not a guess from a requirements doc.

2Fit the workflow, don't replace it

The team kept working the way that already worked. Software moved to meet them, one workflow at a time, while classes kept running.

3Know what developers don't

Payment plans, make-up classes, instructor pay, why a lead goes cold. That knowledge comes from running a business, and it shapes every screen.

4Measured in hours given back

The test for every feature: does a small team get more done this week? Generic tools add work. These remove it.

One student, start to finish

Here's how a single new student moves through the system. Watch who has to type something in.

  1. Finds the school on Google or sees an adWebsite
  2. Takes a 60-second quiz and gets a program recommendationWebsite
  3. Gets scored and receives a welcome text within a minutePanda
  4. Books a campus tour, which shows up on their record by itselfPanda
  5. Pays through a custom payment plan link and is enrolled instantlyWebsite + Panda
  6. Gets a portal login with their classes already waitingStudent Portal
  7. Instructor takes attendance, which also logs their hours for payrollInstructor Portal
  8. Class recording is published and the student gets a textStudent Portal
  9. Their tuition and the ad that found them roll into the quarterly reportPanda

Staff type that student's details in zero times. The only human steps are the ones that need a human: the tour, the teaching, and the conversation.
